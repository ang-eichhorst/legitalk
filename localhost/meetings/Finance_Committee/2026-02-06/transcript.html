Finance Committee - 2026-02-06

Meetings

  • Call to Order and Opening Remarks (00:11:37)
    Chair Horn opens the Finance, Revenue and Bonding Committee session, welcomes Co-Chair Sen. Fonfara and the new OPM Secretary; light remarks about spring and housekeeping.
  • Governor’s FY27 Budget Adjustments – Overview (Revenue/Spending, Guardrails) (00:12:28)
    Secretary Wolczyk presents the Governor’s FY27 budget adjustments focused on affordability and growth; appropriations rise modestly (~$85M; ~0.3%) to $28.7B and remain under the cap; FY27 General Fund revenues reduced by ~$242.6M from enacted due to policy changes yet remain balanced.
  • Technical Pause – Streaming Issue (00:17:12)
    Brief pause to address CTN/Zoom technical issue; meeting resumes with the Secretary continuing the presentation.
  • Affordability Initiatives – Broad-Based Tax Rebate (00:18:47)
    Proposal for a one-time tax rebate of $200 per individual and $400 per joint filer to offset high energy/heating costs, funded by excess volatile revenues, benefiting ~1.6M taxpayers and ~2.2M residents.
  • Affordability Initiatives – Eliminating Professional License Fees (00:19:40)
    Proposal to eliminate selected professional license fees/renewals (varied by profession), easing costs for ~160,000 workers.
  • Hospital Provider Tax and Supplemental Payments Restructuring (00:21:01)
    Adjusts hospital taxes between FY26 and FY27 by +$100M and increases supplemental Medicaid payments by +$140M, yielding a net +$40M benefit to hospitals overall. Federal match on payments produces a ~$53M net state benefit; aligns with updated federal constraints (post-HR1 language).
  • R&D Tax Credit Expansion to Pass-Through Entities (00:22:04)
    Extends the state R&D credit to pass-throughs and makes it refundable, targeting early-stage firms (e.g., biotech/fintech) that may lack current profits; limited General Fund impact due to interaction with volatile E&F revenues.
  • Corporate Tax Conformity – Immediate Deduction for R&E (Prospective Only) (00:23:44)
    Conforms to federal changes allowing immediate deduction of research and experimental expenses starting in 2026; declines to adopt retroactive conformity to protect FY27 revenues.
  • Decoupling from New Federal Bonus Depreciation for Certain Real Property (00:24:39)
    Retains 39-year state deduction period for qualified production real property constructed 1/20/2025–12/31/2028, decoupling from new federal immediate expensing to preserve revenue consistency.
  • Transfers to Bolster the Early Childhood Education Endowment (00:25:41)
    Reduces FY26 General Fund transfer to the Municipal Revenue Sharing Fund by $13.1M (due to higher sales tax receipts) and reduces $30M of FY26 revenue carryforward to FY27, together increasing FY26 surplus to enhance the Early Childhood Endowment by $43M.
  • General Fund Adjustments – Current Services and Policy Changes (00:27:15)
    Adds $73.2M for higher current services in several agencies (e.g., DESPP, Housing, CTECS, Corrections, Judicial) and $67.7M in policy expansions (e.g., universal free breakfast, school-based mental health expansion, literacy coaches, free bus fares for veterans), partially offset by $33.3M in reductions.
  • Special Transportation Fund (STF) Adjustments (00:30:30)
    Reflects increased bus/rail current services (+$30M), health cost updates, a 50% bus fare reduction for students and veterans, and lower-than-expected STF debt service, netting a ~$12.2M reduction in total STF appropriations.
  • Capital Budget – FY27 Authorizations and Priorities (00:32:22)
    Proposes ~$5.7B in authorizations across transportation, education, economic development, infrastructure, and housing. Highlights include a new Windham Technical HS, DOC security upgrades, Grad PLUS subsidized loan support via CHESLA, and $1.5M for a Mesonet weather monitoring system; housing authorizations have tripled vs. prior years.
  • Q&A: Hospital Provider Tax—Net Impacts and Negotiations (00:35:23)
    Chair Horn requests clarity on the +$100M tax increase, the +$140M in supplemental payments, and the ~$53.8M state net. Secretary explains federal rules prevent one-to-one payback, designs ensure all systems are net winners vs. FY26, and notes $15M of room absorbed by Waterbury Hospital (now aligned with UConn Health), leaving ~$25M net among remaining hospitals.
  • Q&A: One-Time Rebates, Volatility Cap, and ADEC Savings (00:45:22)
    Rep. Polletta asks how $200/$400 rebates are funded and the impact on long-term pension savings. Secretary: funded from volatile revenues above the cap; BRF refilled to 18% then excess pays down liabilities. ~$1.6B projected above threshold; $500M for rebates and >$1B still to liabilities; rule of thumb ~$8M ADEC savings per $100M.
  • Q&A: Fiscal Guardrails, Off-Cap Spending, and Rebate Timing (00:52:46)
    Sen. Fazio questions reconciling guardrails with cap changes and off-cap spending. Secretary emphasizes continued large paydowns and sustained surpluses; affirms guardrails’ role. Rebate checks must be issued by Oct 1.
  • Q&A: Education Supports and Veterans’ Bus Fare Relief (00:57:08)
    Rep. McGee asks about free breakfast and behavioral health programs plus veterans’ transportation. Secretary distinguishes universal breakfast from school-based behavioral health clinic expansion; on revenue side, proposes 50% bus fare reduction for students/veterans and a DVA-administered free bus pass option for veterans statewide.
  • Q&A: Mohegan-Pequot Fund Distribution and Coastal Resilience (01:00:50)
    Rep. Bumgardner asks about distribution changes and a noted $1.6M increase. Secretary: it’s a transfer to correctly place two $800k grants for Ledyard and Montville into the Pequot-Mohegan Fund. Also discusses capital prioritization for municipal coastal resilience vs. state facilities.
  • Q&A: STF Debt Service Savings and Reserve Policy (01:05:34)
    Sen. Honeck queries the large STF debt service reduction. OPM’s Brian Testneri explains debt management, timing, and adoption of an 18% reserve policy similar to the General Fund, using excess to defease debt (~$500M in 2023), yielding ~$60–$70M in reduced debt service.
  • Q&A: Grayfield Program Bonding Prospects (01:07:40)
    Rep. Napoli highlights the Grayfield program for reusing vacant retail into housing/tax base. OPM is open to working on priorities but has not set the next bonding agenda yet.
  • Q&A: Rebate Eligibility for Non-Filers and Early Childhood Endowment Deposit (01:09:06)
    Rep. Farrar asks about eligibility for residents not required to file and the total endowment deposit. Secretary: many low-income workers still file due to EITC; some low-income retirees on Social Security might be missed—open to discussing a fix. Anticipated endowment deposit is ~$130M ($87M EOY surplus + $43M transfers).
  • Q&A: STF Outlook and Health Care Affordability Measures (01:13:22)
    Sen. Hwang asks about STF deficits and options; Secretary says FY27 is balanced but a broader sustainability plan is forthcoming. On health care, outlines state exchange subsidies, a proposed small-employer tax credit matching ICHRA contributions (up to $1,000/employee), gig-worker benefit accounts, and a Connecticut Option plan (publicly designed, privately administered) to expand affordable choices.
  • Q&A: School Breakfast Detail and Transit Fares for Students/Seniors/Veterans (01:22:57)
    Rep. Rader (Vice Chair) seeks details on reduced-price student meal coverage and bus fare policy. OPM to follow up on breakfast specifics and notes that fully free student/senior bus fares are not included due to federal DOT constraints; proposes 50% reduction and veterans’ free pass approach instead.
  • Q&A: Occupational License Fee Relief – Selection Rationale (01:26:04)
    Chair Horn asks how occupations were selected for fee relief. Secretary: aimed for a broad-based, practical set across working trades to reduce recurring costs and improve affordability.
  • Closing Thanks (01:27:21)
    Chair thanks the Secretary and OPM team; acknowledges members’ competing committee obligations and ongoing collaboration.
