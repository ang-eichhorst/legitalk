Legitalk:

Aging Committee - 2026-02-05

Meetings

  • Call to order and opening remarks (00:00:00)
    Sen. Jan Hochadel convened the Aging Committee; Rep. Jane Garibay, Sen. Tony Hwang, and Rep. Mitch Bolinsky offered welcoming remarks emphasizing bipartisan collaboration and priorities for seniors.
  • Overview: Concepts to be raised (Items 1–11) (00:03:15)
    Chairs listed 11 concepts to raise, including: virtual visitation/monitoring in nursing and residential care homes; excluding spousal income for certain cost-sharing; training to prevent retaliation and for homemaker-companion agencies; studies of long-term care needs and seniors' needs; municipal agents for aging; restricting private equity ownership of nursing homes; DSS recommendations on bed moratorium exceptions, resident data, and reimbursement; a family caregiver tax credit; and rate increases at assisted living facilities.
  • Motion and voice vote: Raise Items 2–7 and 9–11 (00:05:59)
    After withdrawing an initial motion to separate items, the committee moved to raise Items 2–7 and 9–11 together. Rep. Anne Hughes noted potential inclusion of a senior abuse/neglect protection concept. Motion passed by voice vote.
  • Discussion and roll call vote: Act restricting private equity ownership of nursing homes (Item 8) (00:07:04)
    Members debated whether to restrict versus improve oversight of private equity in nursing homes. Rep. Jay Case opposed, citing facility needs and reimbursement shortfalls; Rep. Bolinsky urged focusing on proactive oversight; Rep. Pizzuto and Rep. DiGiovancarlo voiced concerns informed by Waterbury/Prospect Medical experiences. Chair Garibay clarified the concept seeks solutions, not a ban. Sen. Hwang supported raising the concept while emphasizing robust public hearing and oversight. A roll call vote was held; the concept was raised with several no votes.
  • Discussion and voice vote: Use of technology for virtual visitation and monitoring in nursing and residential care homes (Item 1) (00:17:38)
    Rep. Jay Case raised privacy/HIPAA and roommate-consent concerns and past difficulty securing video feeds. Chair Garibay and others emphasized benefits for transparency, safety, and family connection, acknowledging the need for safeguards. Sen. Hwang supported exploring technology to reduce isolation while protecting privacy. The concept was raised on a voice vote.
  • Announcements and acknowledgments (00:26:31)
    Chairs announced upcoming screening and committee meetings, held votes open until noon/House session, and recognized committee staff, including new clerk Liz Aart and policy/analyst teams.
  • Discussion: Elder caregiver abuse registry concept; reference to HB 5779 (01:47:41)
    An extended Zoom segment addressed a proposal to create a registry/list to screen caregivers, with concerns about due process, public posting of personal information, and scope. A participant referenced 'fifty seven seventy nine,' normalized as HB 5779, concerning a study/penalties for elder abuse. Questions covered how the public and agencies would access any database and alternatives such as enhanced background checks and penalties.
